Blue solid: charged tracks (100 MeV e⁻ crossing; electron knocked out of the mesh). Orange dashed: photons, one converting in the gas, one in the mesh wire. Electrons drift down to the mesh, multiply in the 150 µm gap, the pads collect the signal.

A photon is detected if its conversion puts at least one electron into the drift gas: either it converts in the gas itself, or it converts in a solid (mostly the steel mesh, also the copper) and the knocked-out electron escapes into the gas. Above about 40 keV in argon, and above about 15 keV in neon, the second kind dominates.
