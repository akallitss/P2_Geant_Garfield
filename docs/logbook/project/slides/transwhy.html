Why the gases differ: how hot the electrons are

  • Field lines all pass the mesh; electrons are lost when they diffuse off the edge of a funnel onto the top of a wire, in the last ~50 µm
  • Ar and Ne collide only elastically below 11.5 / 16.6 eV and lose ~0.003 % of their energy per collision: the field heats the electrons
  • Isobutane (and CO2, CF4) absorbs energy in molecular vibrations at 0.1–0.4 eV: it cools them. 5 % is not enough, 10–15 % is
  • Sideways spread over the last 50 µm, σ ≈ D_T·√L: ~35 µm in Ar/iso 95/5 (half the 68 µm wire spacing), ~19 µm in Ne/iso 85/15
  • Handles: more quencher, or a lower drift field (cooler electrons and wider funnels)
ε_k = e·D_T/μ = D_T²·E/2, from the Magboltz tables at 638 V/cm (thermal: 0.025 eV). The two hottest mixtures agree within one standard deviation. Argon's Ramsauer minimum (~0.2 eV) gives long free flights, which is also why the stepping correction mattered most for Ar/iso 95/5.
